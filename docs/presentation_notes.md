# Presentation Notes for the Cyclistic Dashboard

Approximate presentation time: **3–4 minutes**

## Opening

> This analysis compares Cyclistic casual riders and annual members over October 2025 through September 2026. The main finding is that casual rides show stronger leisure-oriented patterns, while member rides are more consistent with regular transportation.

## 1. Monthly seasonality

> Both groups are seasonal, but casual ridership is substantially more seasonal. Casual rides fall very sharply during winter and increase strongly toward summer. Member usage also increases in warmer months, but the variation is less extreme. This suggests casual use is more dependent on season and favorable riding conditions.

## 2. Weekday versus weekend

> The weekly pattern shows almost opposite behavior. Casual riders average about **5,257 rides per weekday** and **7,854 per weekend day**, roughly a **49% increase**. Members average about **11,708 rides per weekday** but only **8,913 per weekend day**, roughly a **24% decrease**. This is one of the clearest differences between the two groups.

## 3. Ride duration

> Casual rides also last substantially longer. Their average valid ride duration is about **17.6 minutes**, compared with about **11.9 minutes for members**. Members therefore make more rides overall, but casual trips tend to be longer.

## 4. Time of day

> The hourly pattern provides another distinction. Member rides show a clear morning peak around **8 AM** and a stronger late-afternoon peak around **5 PM**. Casual usage rises more gradually through the day and is more concentrated in the afternoon. These patterns are consistent with commuting or routine transportation among members and more discretionary use among casual riders. However, the dataset does not directly record trip purpose, so this is an interpretation rather than proof.

## 5. Station geography

> The station analysis reinforces that interpretation. Tourist and lakefront locations skew strongly casual: for example, about **75.8% of rides starting at Navy Pier** and **78.6% at DuSable Lake Shore Drive & Monroe Street** are casual. In contrast, downtown stations such as **Canal & Madison** and **Clinton & Jackson** are strongly member-oriented. Because station information is missing for some rides, this geographic conclusion applies only to rides with station data.

## Closing

> Taken together, the important result is not one individual metric but the consistency across several independent patterns: seasonality, weekends, ride duration, time of day, and geography all distinguish casual rides from member rides. This gives Cyclistic a useful behavioral basis for developing marketing strategies aimed at converting casual riders to annual memberships.
